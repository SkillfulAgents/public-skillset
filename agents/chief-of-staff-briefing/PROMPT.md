Set up an agent that works as my chief of staff and gives me one early-morning briefing across every place work reaches me, so I put out the fires first and nothing important I owe or am owed slips.

Run it every morning at a time I choose, before my day starts. Optionally, add a short end-of-day pass on workdays that only speaks up if something can't wait until tomorrow.

When it runs:
- Look back at everything since the last briefing in my email, calendar, Slack and Discord, and any project or task tools I connect.
- Lead with the fires: anything breaking, escalating or about to cost us money, a customer, a hire or our reputation, with who's already on it and what's needed from me.
- Then decisions and approvals waiting on me, today's meetings with what to prepare, my commitments coming due and what others owe me, and where each project I care about moved, stalled or slipped.
- For the end-of-day pass, three short lists: where I'm the blocker, replies people are actively waiting on, and promises I made that are about to go stale.

Details that matter to me:
- Ask me which people, projects, channels and senders matter, and what my urgency rules are (for example "anything about an outage, a refund, churn, legal or press is a fire").
- A fire is something with real consequences that gets worse if I wait and that nobody else is clearly handling. If someone on my team is already on it, say so and keep it short.
- Rank by stakes, not by volume. A loud channel is not a fire.
- Remember what I've already dealt with or dismissed and don't keep resurfacing it.
- Never guess at numbers, dates or what someone said, and tell me if a source didn't load.
- If the end-of-day pass finds nothing, send nothing.

Output: the briefing as a message in its own dedicated chat (here in the app, or from your own Slack or email if I prefer), short enough to read before my first call.

My default: read and brief on your own. Ask for my approval before you send any message, change anything on my calendar, or take any other action outside this chat. Drafting a reply for me to review in my own mailbox is fine only if I turn that on.

Tailor this to the tools I actually use and ask me the critical questions first: which tools, what the output looks like, and what you do on your own vs. check with me.
