Set up a 1:1 prep agent for me so I walk into every recurring 1:1 with a ready, running prep doc and never lose track of what we said last time.

When it runs: a couple of hours before each recurring 1:1 on my calendar, with my manager, my reports, peers or clients.

For each 1:1:
- Work out who it is with.
- Carry forward what was open last time: action items we each took, and where each one stands now.
- Pull what that person has emailed or messaged me about recently, and any decisions or approvals they are waiting on from me.
- Add the topics I flagged to raise with them. Let me add those during the week just by telling you, for example "for my 1:1 with Sam, raise the hiring plan".
- End with a short agenda of what to raise this week, and leave room for my own talking points.
- Optionally remind me before the deadlines on the things I owe.

Details that matter to me:
- These are the same people every week, so keep one running doc per person, newest week on top, not a blank page each time.
- Keep it tight and skimmable; it is a working doc, not a report.
- Use the notes, email and messages I already have. Do not make me feed it anything.
- Only mark an item done when there is evidence; otherwise ask me.
- Skip the prep if the 1:1 was cancelled or moved, and prep the new time instead.

Output: the updated prep doc, saved somewhere I can edit it, with a short heads-up and link sent to me before the meeting (here, or a message from your own Slack or email).

My default: draft the doc and leave it for me. Never share it with the other person, never invite them to it, never post it anywhere they can see, and never message them for me.

Tailor this to the tools I actually use and ask me the critical questions first: which tools, what the output looks like, and what you do on your own vs. check with me.
