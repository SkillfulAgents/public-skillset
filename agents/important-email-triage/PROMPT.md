Set up an agent that quietly labels the email genuinely waiting on me through the day, so a glance at one label tells me what needs me and nothing important hides under the noise.

Check my inbox as new mail arrives, or every 30 minutes if that's not possible, and let me run it on demand against today's unread.

When it runs:
- Look at each new message and decide whether a real person is waiting on me: a question, a request, an approval, a decision, a deadline.
- Label those as waiting on me, and sort the rest into my own categories, learned from my real mail and named the way I'd name them.
- Ignore newsletters, receipts, notifications and cold outreach.
- Give me one short list, newest first, with who it is and what each person is asking for.

Details that matter to me:
- When a message is ambiguous, leave it alone rather than guessing. A wrong label is worse than none.
- Never label the same thread twice, and take the label off once I've replied or someone else picked it up.
- Anyone I tell you always matters goes on the list, unless it's clearly automated.
- Cold outreach that pretends to be a reply ("Re:", "following up on my last note") is still cold outreach.
- Tell me what you notice that I might not: senders I always ignore, threads I keep leaving hanging.
- Only message me when something new got labeled. No empty updates.
- If I want it, use investor-style buckets instead: Today's Focus, Action Required, Scheduling, Delegated, with one line on why each Today's Focus item is urgent. A term sheet or signed document is always Today's Focus, and fund-admin and LP mail stays separate from deal flow.

Output: labels in my inbox, plus a short list of what's newly waiting on me, sent from your own Slack or email or shown here in the app.

My default: label on your own, but never send, draft, archive, delete or unsubscribe from anything.

Tailor this to the tools I actually use and ask me the critical questions first: which tools, what the output looks like, and what you do on your own vs. check with me.
