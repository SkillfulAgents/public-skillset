Set up an agent that shows me, once a day, only the messages I actually owe a reply to across my email and Slack, with a reply already drafted in my voice, so nothing important slips and I never have to dig for it.

Run it once each weekday morning at a time I pick, and let me ask for it on demand.

When it runs:
- Look at my work email and my Slack direct messages and mentions since the last run, plus anything still open from the past week.
- Keep only the threads where someone is genuinely waiting on me: a direct question, a request, a customer or prospect thread, a renewal or pricing question, an intro, a commitment I haven't closed.
- For each email, write a proposed reply in my voice and leave it as a draft in my own mailbox. For Slack, put a suggested reply I can paste.
- Send me one list, each item with who it is, a one-line reason it needs me, how long they've waited, and a link straight to the message.

Details that matter to me:
- Keep the bar high. Skip newsletters, automated notifications, receipts, calendar invitations and replies, and internal noise.
- Skip threads where I was the last to respond, where a colleague already answered for me, or where someone else clearly owns the next step.
- Email and Slack go in one list so I check one place, ordered by what's most time-sensitive or has waited longest.
- Learn how I write from my sent mail: my greetings, sign-off, length and tone. Short and plain beats polished.
- Anyone I tell you always counts as important goes near the top.
- Don't show me the same thread every day unchanged; mark it as a repeat or drop it once I've dealt with it.
- Never invent facts, prices, dates or promises in a draft. If you don't know, leave a clear blank for me to fill.
- If nothing needs a reply, send nothing at all.

Output: one short digest, sent as a direct message from your own Slack or shown here in the app, plus reply drafts waiting in my mailbox.

My default: read, sort and draft on your own, but never send a reply, post in Slack as me, or change anything in my mailbox beyond saving drafts.

Tailor this to the tools I actually use and ask me the critical questions first: which tools, what the output looks like, and what you do on your own vs. check with me.
