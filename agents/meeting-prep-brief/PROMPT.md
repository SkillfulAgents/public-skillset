Set up a meeting prep agent for me so I walk into every meeting that matters already knowing who is in the room, where we left things, and the one thing I should say or ask.

When it runs: for each meeting that deserves prep (external, first-time or high-stakes), either the evening before or shortly before the call, whichever I pick. Skip internal-only meetings and the routine 1:1s I run on autopilot.

For each meeting:
- Lead with the one thing I should say or ask, and why.
- Research who is in the room: role, background, what they have said or posted lately, and their company's recent news.
- Read my email history with them, plus any past call notes, CRM records or Slack threads I have connected.
- Pull what each side promised last time, open items first, and say whether they were done.
- Note what is new since we last spoke: a role change, a funding round, a launch, a thread they are waiting on me for.
- If it is a new account with no history, do the research online instead.

Details that matter to me:
- Tell internal meetings apart by the attendees' email domains, so I do not get briefs for team syncs.
- Set the depth by the meeting type: a first meeting needs the full who-are-they picture, a routine check-in needs only what changed.
- Keep it short and skimmable on my phone. If you are not sure about a fact, say so; never guess a title or a number.
- If someone moved, cancelled or rescheduled, brief the new time once, not twice.
- If I have a slides agent, you may ask it for a tailored deck for calls that need one, but check with me first.

Output: one short brief per meeting, delivered where I choose (in this chat, a message from your own Slack or email, or my inbox), timed so it is fresh when I read it.

My default: research and brief on your own. Never contact an attendee, never accept, decline or change a calendar event, and never send anything on my behalf.

Tailor this to the tools I actually use and ask me the critical questions first: which tools, what the output looks like, and what you do on your own vs. check with me.
